Which Speech Representation Better Matches Text-Native Reasoning?

A study of speech–text alignment on frame rate and representation

AMCC 5160 · Weekly Presentation · Xinshen Zhang, co-author

Background: the speech–text modality gap

Spoken dialogue models start from text LLMs — but reasoning degrades on speech input

  • Typical fix: fine-tune the whole backbone on speech / speech–text data
  • Costly, and it entangles two things: better speech representation vs. a changed LLM
This paper's choice: freeze the text LLM (Qwen3-4B / 8B) and treat speech token design as the only variable, with the information rate fixed at 600 bits/s.

Redundant positions → diluted per-token semantic density, attention spread thin, text-native reasoning dynamics perturbed.

Why not just lower the frame rate?

Single codebook + single LM head → information bottleneck (ASR test-other WER, Whisper features + FSQ)

  • 4k codebook (2¹²): breaks below 12.5 Hz
  • 256k codebook (2¹⁸): breaks below ~6 Hz
  • Bigger vocabulary ⇒ bigger LM head + softmax — not scalable
Ours keeps WER at 6–8% all the way down to 2.08 Hz.

Method ①: Factorized FSQ + NAR audio head

Constant bitrate R = r · n · log₂K — lower frame rate ⇒ more groups per frame

  • Factorized FSQ. Not one giant codebook. Split the dimensions into n groups. Each group is a 4,096-way prediction, all in parallel.
  • NAR audio head. Two-layer Transformer. Hidden state plus a learnable slot per group. Groups attend before classification. The LLM stays frozen.
  • Bitrate stays constant. Half the frame rate, twice the bits per frame. At 4.17 Hz that is 144 bits, 12 groups.

Understanding tolerates redundancy, generation does not → QA is best where both work. At 50 Hz, ASR/TTS are fine but QA is only 5.0 vs 23.3 at 4.17 Hz.

Why 4.17 Hz, not exactly the text rate (3.32 Hz)?

Probe: stretch Qwen3-4B's text embeddings by a factor, measure MMLU

  • MMLU stays > 50% for stretch 0.8×–2.2×; collapses below 0.8×
  • 3.32 Hz is an average: fast speakers / short utterances fall below 0.8× text length
  • 4.17 Hz & 6.25 Hz keep almost all utterances inside the window
Design rule: keep speech/text length ratio inside the LLM's tolerance window across the whole distribution, not just the mean.

Method ②: contrastive alignment at intermediate layers

Length alignment fixes structure; representation alignment closes the semantic gap

For each speech–transcript pair: take frozen-LLM hidden states at layer ℓ, mean-pool over time, L2-normalize.

InfoNCE (τ = 0.07, λ = 0.1) pulls paired speech/text together, pushes other in-batch pairs apart.

Gradient flows only into projector + audio head — LLM stays frozen.

Applied in all 3 stages: S2T → T2S → S2S QA

Mid-layer: 23.3 → 30.7 (+32% relative). Embedding-level alignment slightly hurts.

Takeaways, limits, and questions

Lessons
  • Frame rate is a first-class design variable (up to 4× QA gap)
  • Aim ~1.2–2× the mean text rate
  • Align at the middle layer
Limitations
  • Only Qwen3, English, read speech
  • Frozen LLM may cap performance
  • No acoustic / paralinguistic modeling
  • Small benchmarks, GPT-4o judge, no error bars
For discussion
  • Would the optimum shift for Chinese (tokens/s differs) or for conversational speech?
  • After LoRA / unfreezing, does the best frame rate still sit near the text rate?
  • Fixed bitrate: are frame rate and bits/frame really separable?
  • Is "match text length" the right principle — or just a proxy for something else?
